---
Under Section 8 of the Hindu Succession Act, 1956, when a Hindu male dies intestate, his estate devolves primarily upon the heirs specified in Class I of the Schedule. If there are no surviving Class I heirs, the property devolves next upon the heirs specified in Class II. In the absence of both Class I and Class II heirs, succession passes to the agnates of the deceased, and failing any agnates, to his cognates.

---
Section 9 establishes that Class I heirs take the property simultaneously to the total exclusion of all other relatives. Among Class II heirs, relatives listed in the first entry take precedence and exclude those in later entries; relatives grouped together within the same entry take simultaneously in equal shares.

clause_id: HSA-1956-S10

jurisdiction: "India"
topic: "Distribution of Property Among Class I Heirs"
needs_review: true
official_url: "https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/1622"
---
Section 10 defines the division rules among Class I heirs:
1. The surviving spouse takes one equal share.
2. The surviving mother takes one equal share.
3. Each surviving son and daughter takes one equal share.
4. The heirs of any predeceased child take collectively one branch share (representation per stirpes), distributed equally among the children and surviving spouse of that predeceased child.
The deceased father is not a Class I heir and receives no share when Class I heirs survive.

clause_id: HSA-1956-SCHEDULE
source: "Hindu Succession Act, 1956"
section: "Schedule"
jurisdiction: "India"
topic: "Classification of Statutory Heirs into Class I and Class II"
needs_review: true
official_url: "https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/1622"
---
The Schedule to the Hindu Succession Act categorizes heirs:
- Class I Heirs: Son, daughter, widow, mother, son of a predeceased son, daughter of a predeceased son, widow of a predeceased son, son of a predeceased daughter, daughter of a predeceased daughter, and other lineal descendants through predeceased branches.
- Class II Heirs: Entry I comprises the Father. Entry II comprises Son's daughter's son, son's daughter's daughter, brother, and sister. Subsequent entries encompass grandparents, uncles, aunts, and collateral relations.

clause_id: HSA-1956-S6

jurisdiction: "India"
topic: "Devolution of Interest in Coparcenary Property"
needs_review: true

---
Section 6 provides that daughters of a coparcener in a Hindu Undivided Family become coparceners by birth with equal rights, liabilities, and status as sons. On the death of a Hindu coparcener, his undivided share in joint family property is determined by a deemed notional partition immediately preceding his demise, and his resulting separate share devolves by testamentary disposition or intestate succession under Section 8.

Under Section 14, any property acquired by a female Hindu—whether by inheritance, partition, gift, purchase, or maintenance—is held by her as absolute owner with full powers of disposal, extinguishing traditional limited estate doctrines.

---
clause_id: HSA-1956-S15
source: "Hindu Succession Act, 1956"
section: "15"
jurisdiction: "India"
topic: "General Rules of Succession in the Case of Female Hindus"
needs_review: true
official_url: "https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/1622"
---
Section 15 sets the devolution order for an intestate female Hindu:
1. Firstly, upon her sons, daughters (including children of any predeceased son or daughter), and husband.
2. Secondly, upon the heirs of the husband.
3. Thirdly, upon the mother and father.
However, property inherited from her parents reverts to her father's heirs in the absence of any surviving children, and property inherited from her husband or father-in-law reverts to her husband's heirs.

Under Section 18, heirs related to an intestate by full blood (descended from a common ancestor by the same wife) are preferred over heirs related by half blood (common ancestor by different wives), provided the nature of relationship is otherwise identical.

Section 25 disqualifies any person who commits murder or abets the commission of murder of the deceased from inheriting any property of the victim, or any property in furtherance of the succession to which he committed or abetted the crime.

jurisdiction: "India"
topic: "Succession Rights of Descendants of Converts"
needs_review: true
official_url: "https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/1622"
---
Section 26 provides that where a Hindu has ceased to be a Hindu by conversion to another religion, children born to that convert after the conversion and their descendants are disqualified from inheriting the property of any Hindu relative, unless they have reconverted to Hinduism prior to succession opening.

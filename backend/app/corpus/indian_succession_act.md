jurisdiction: "India"
topic: "Testamentary Capacity to Dispose of Property by Will"
needs_review: true
official_url: "https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/2385"
---
Under Section 59 of the Indian Succession Act, 1925, every person of sound mind not being a minor may dispose of their property by will. Testamentary capacity requires that the testator understands the nature of the act, the extent of the property being disposed of, and the claims of individuals whom they are including or excluding from the testamentary bequest.

clause_id: ISA-1925-WILL-FORMALITIES
source: "Indian Succession Act, 1925"
section: "63"
jurisdiction: "India"
topic: "Execution and Attestation Formalities for Valid Wills"
needs_review: true
official_url: "https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/2385"
---
Section 63 prescribes the mandatory execution formalities for unprivileged wills:
1. The testator must sign or affix their mark to the will, or have another person sign in their presence and by their direction.
2. The signature or mark must clearly demonstrate intent to give effect to the writing as a will.
3. The will must be attested by two or more credible witnesses, each of whom must have seen the testator sign or received a personal acknowledgment, and each witness must sign in the presence of the testator. Simultaneous attestation is not strictly mandatory, but both must sign before probate.

clause_id: ISA-1925-PROBATE
source: "Indian Succession Act, 1925"
section: "213"
jurisdiction: "India"
topic: "Requirement of Probate for Establishing Right as Executor or Legatee"
needs_review: true
official_url: "https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/2385"
---
Section 213 provides that no right as executor or legatee under a will can be established in any court of justice unless a court of competent jurisdiction has granted probate of the will or letters of administration with the will annexed. In the case of Hindu wills, this mandatory requirement applies primarily to wills executed within the territories of the ordinary original civil jurisdiction of the High Courts of Calcutta, Madras, and Bombay, or wills executed outside those areas relating to immovable property situated within them.

clause_id: ISA-1925-SUCCESSION-CERT
source: "Indian Succession Act, 1925"
section: "370"
jurisdiction: "India"
topic: "Succession Certificate for Debts and Securities"
needs_review: true
official_url: "https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/2385"
---
Under Part X (Section 370 onwards) of the Indian Succession Act, 1925, a district court or competent civil court may grant a succession certificate in respect of debts and securities owing to a deceased person. A succession certificate provides legal protection to debtors or financial institutions paying debts or transferring securities to the holder of the certificate, indemnifying the debtor against conflicting heir claims.

clause_id: ISA-1925-LETTER-OF-ADMIN

jurisdiction: "India"
topic: "Grant of Letters of Administration on Intestacy"
needs_review: true
official_url: "https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/2385"
---
Under Section 218, where a deceased person has died intestate without leaving a testamentary appointment of an executor, letters of administration to the estate may be granted to any person who, according to the rules for the distribution of the estate applicable in the case of such deceased, would be entitled to the whole or any part of such deceased's estate.

clause_id: ISA-1925-REVOCATION-WILL
source: "Indian Succession Act, 1925"
section: "62"
jurisdiction: "India"
topic: "Revocability of Will During Testator Lifetime"
needs_review: true
official_url: "https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/2385"
---
Section 62 establishes that a will is inherently ambulatory and revocable at any time during the testator's lifetime. A will may be revoked by the execution of a subsequent valid testamentary instrument, by a writing declaring an intention to revoke executed in the manner of a will, or by burning, tearing, or otherwise destroying the instrument with the intention of revoking it.

clause_id: ISA-1925-EXEC-DUTIES
source: "Indian Succession Act, 1925"
section: "317"
jurisdiction: "India"
topic: "Duties of an Executor in Inventory and Administration of Estate"
needs_review: true
official_url: "https://www.indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/2385"
---
Section 317 outlines the statutory obligations of an executor or administrator:
1. Within six months from the grant of probate or letters of administration, an executor must exhibit in court a full and true inventory of all property in possession and debts owing to the estate.
2. Within one year from the grant, the executor must render an account of the estate showing asset collections, discharge of debts, and distribution of bequests.

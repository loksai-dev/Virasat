topic: "Settlement of Deceased Account Claims With Registered Nomination"
needs_review: true
official_url: "https://www.rbi.org.in"
---
Under Reserve Bank of India instructions on customer service and deceased depositor accounts, when an account has a valid registered nominee or survivorship mandate (such as Either or Survivor / Former or Survivor), banks are directed to release account balances to the nominee or surviving holder upon verification of the depositor's death certificate and the claimant's KYC identification. Banks must not insist on production of a succession certificate, probate, letter of administration, or indemnity bond from the nominee, provided there is no conflicting court order restraining payment.

jurisdiction: "India"
topic: "Settlement of Deceased Account Claims Without Nomination"
needs_review: true
official_url: "https://www.rbi.org.in"
---
Where a deceased depositor has not registered a nomination and the account has no survivorship mandate, banks follow a board-approved threshold policy for simplified settlement. Below the bank-specific threshold limit, claims are settled on the basis of a joint claim application by all legal heirs, death certificate, legal heir certificate or family tree affidavit, and an indemnity bond with sureties. For claims exceeding the threshold limit, banks may require formal legal representation such as a succession certificate or letters of administration unless all heirs execute a joint claim with acceptable sureties.

---
RBI directives mandate that commercial banks settle claims in respect of deceased depositors and release funds to the nominee, survivor, or legal heirs within a maximum period of 15 days from the date of receipt of all required claim documents, KYC proofs, and application forms. Banks must maintain branch claim registers and acknowledge receipt of claim applications in writing.

clause_id: RBI-DECEASED-CLAIMS-DOCUMENTS

jurisdiction: "India"
topic: "Documentation Requirements for Deceased Account Settlement"
needs_review: true
official_url: "https://www.rbi.org.in"
---
Standard documentation prescribed by banks under RBI guidance for deceased depositor claim settlement includes:
1. Formal claim application form signed by the claimant (nominee, survivor, or all legal heirs).
2. Original or certified copy of the death certificate issued by municipal or local authorities.
3. KYC documents (identity and address proof) of the claimant(s).
4. Unused cheque leaves, passbook, or fixed deposit receipts.
5. In accounts without nomination: legal heirship certificate, affidavit of legal heirs, and indemnity bond signed by all heirs and sureties.

section: ""
jurisdiction: "India"
topic: "Access to Safe Deposit Lockers With Nomination"
needs_review: true
official_url: "https://www.rbi.org.in"
---
Where a safe deposit locker was hired with a registered nomination or survivorship clause, the bank must allow access to the locker and permit removal of contents to the nominee or surviving hirer upon verifying the death certificate and claimant identity. The bank must prepare an exhaustive inventory of the locker contents in the presence of the nominee and two independent witnesses before handing over the articles.

topic: "Access to Safe Deposit Lockers Without Nomination"
needs_review: true

---
In safe deposit lockers without nomination or survivorship clauses, access and release of contents require the joint attendance and unanimous written request of all recognized legal heirs. An inventory is compiled in the presence of all heirs, independent witnesses, and bank officers. Where disputes exist among heirs, access is frozen pending a court succession certificate or partition order.

clause_id: BANK-NOMINATION-GENERAL
source: "Supreme Court Jurisprudence and Banking Regulations"
section: ""
jurisdiction: "India"
topic: "Nominee as Custodian and Trustee Versus Ownership by Succession"
needs_review: true
official_url: "https://www.rbi.org.in"
---
Under settled Indian law established by the Supreme Court (including Ram Chander Talwar v. Devinder Kumar Talwar and Sarbati Devi), a bank nominee is merely a designated custodian or trustee empowered to receive proceeds and grant a valid discharge to the bank. Nomination does not create title, beneficial ownership, or testamentary succession in favour of the nominee. The funds received by a nominee remain part of the estate of the deceased and are subject to the claims of the statutory legal heirs under personal succession law or beneficiaries under a valid will.
